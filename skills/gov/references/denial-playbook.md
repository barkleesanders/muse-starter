## Denial playbook: "unclear" / "burdensome" denials — duty to assist (added 2026-10-07)

When a NY agency denies a FOIL request as unclear, overly broad, or burdensome — or stonewalls a request it could have clarified — deploy the duty-to-assist theory. Researched 2026-10-06 (six query sets, every source read primary). See also the no-certification playbook above; the two combine.

- **Assist and confer (21 NYCRR 1401.2(b)(2)–(3)).** The records access officer must help the requester identify records and explain how records are filed, retrieved, or generated; when a request is voluminous or locating records requires substantial effort, the agency must CONTACT the requester to narrow it. Demand this in writing: the officer confers with the requester, not denies.
- **Require the certification (1401.2(b)(7)).** If the agency claims no responsive records or non-custodianship after diligent search, demand the written certification — a bare "maintains no records" letter is the defect to name.
- **Burden and enforcement cites.** *Data Tree LLC v. Romaine* (NY Ct. App. 2007): the agency bears a "particularized and specific justification" burden for every denial; exemptions narrowly construed. *Romero v. Morales* (Sup. Ct. N.Y. County, 2007): years of silence without seeking clarification, then telling the petitioner to start over, loses — the court ordered the request processed as reasonably described. Failure to engage is itself the violation.
- **Fee-shifting leverage (POL §89(4)(c)).** A court SHALL award reasonable attorney's fees when the requester substantially prevails and the agency had "no reasonable basis" for the denial; MAY award fees for blown statutory deadlines. Document every dodge, every unanswered clarification, every missing certification — that is the "no reasonable basis" record.
- **Combined theory, operational:** (1) demand assist-and-confer under 1401.2(b)(2)–(3); (2) demand the 1401.2(b)(7) certification; (3) preserve timing and exemption defects for appeal; (4) two-track — appeal the original denial AND file a separate meta-framed request. Exhaust administrative appeals before Article 78.
- **Honest limits.** *Matter of Rattley v. NYPD* (96 NY2d 873): courts defer on HOW agencies search, so "the records must exist" alone loses — win on missing certifications, failure to assist, blown deadlines, unsupported exemptions. No reliable statewide win rate; don't promise one. Pro se requesters generally cannot recover fees for their own time.

Romaine (https://law.justia.com/cases/new-york/court-of-appeals/2007/2007-09906.html), POL §89(4)(c) fee explainer (https://www.nycbar.org/blogs/recovering-attorneys-fees-on-denied-foil-requests/).
